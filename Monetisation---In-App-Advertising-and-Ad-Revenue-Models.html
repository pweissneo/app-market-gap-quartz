How the Model Works

In-app advertising monetises attention rather than direct purchases. The app displays ads from a network or exchange and earns revenue per impression (CPM), per click (CPC), or per conversion (CPA/CPI). Revenue scales with user base size, session depth, and ad format selection.

Revenue flows:

User installs free app
       ↓
App displays ads (banner, interstitial, rewarded video, native)
       ↓
Ad network / exchange auctions impression in real-time (RTB)
       ↓
Advertiser pays eCPM (effective cost per 1,000 impressions)
       ↓
Revenue split: app ~70%, ad network ~30% (varies by network and volume)

Key performance metric: eCPM — effective cost per 1,000 impressions. This is the primary benchmark for ad revenue quality, not raw impression count.

The single biggest factor influencing revenue is ad format. Formats ranked by eCPM:

Critical insight: A single waterfall configuration applied to both iOS and Android loses 20–40% potential revenue. Platform-specific waterfall optimisation is the highest-leverage improvement for any app running ads.

Platform gap: iOS consistently delivers higher eCPMs than Android due to stronger advertiser demand and higher average user purchasing power. However, Android dominates install volume (~60–70% globally). Diversified publishers run separate waterfall configurations per platform.

ApproachProsCons
Single ad network (AdMob, Unity Ads, AppLovin)Simple integration; fill rate guaranteedLimited competition = lower eCPMs

Hybrid waterfallMaximises revenue across demand sourcesComplex to configure and maintain

2. Frequency and UX Balance

  • Interstitial frequency: 1 per 3–5 minutes of active use is the established sweet spot. Too aggressive → retention drop. Too conservative → revenue left on table.
  • Rewarded video best practice: Position as a user choice — watch an ad to unlock content, extend features, or earn virtual currency. This transforms a nuisance into a feature.
  • Native ad integration: Must match the visual language of surrounding content. Poorly integrated native ads feel like ads; well-integrated ones feel like recommendations.

3. Hybrid Monetisation

Apps that combine ads + subscriptions + IAP generate ~30% higher LTV than single-stream models (AppVerticals, 2026). Common patterns:

  • Freemium + ads: Free tier monetised by ads; premium removes ads
  • Subscription + ads: Low-tier subscription still shows ads; premium tier is ad-free
  • IAP + ads: Game progression gated by IAP; optional ad-watching for F2P users

4. First-Party Data Advantage

Apps with strong behavioural data generate up to 2x higher ad revenue than those relying on third-party targeting (AppVerticals, 2026). This is increasingly important as IDFA restrictions and cookie deprecation reduce third-party signal quality.

The Gap: Non-Gaming Apps Undermonetise Ads Systematically

Despite advertising being the largest mobile revenue stream, evidence suggests systematic underperformance outside gaming:

  1. Most non-gaming apps use a single ad network rather than running a waterfall with multiple demand sources, leaving 30–50% revenue unrealised.

  2. Platform-specific optimisation is the exception, not the rule. Most apps apply identical configurations to iOS and Android despite eCPM gaps of 20–40%.

  3. Format selection is often driven by simplicity rather than revenue maximisation. Banner ads are easiest to implement but generate 5–20x less eCPM than rewarded video.

  4. No systematic eCPM benchmarking. Most app developers do not know whether their eCPMs are above or below market average for their category and region.

  5. AI-powered dynamic paywalls and ad placement — proven to improve conversion 15–25% — remain rare outside large publishers.
