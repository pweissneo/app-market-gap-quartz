Monetisation - Licensing Enterprise And White-Label

Enterprise and white-label licensing converts a consumer app into B2B infrastructure. A health system licenses a care coordination app and deploys it to 50,000 patients under their own brand. An employer licenses a wellness app and offers it as an employee benefit. A government agency licenses an app and deploys it across a state. In each case, the app developer gets a recurring enterprise contract; the licensee gets a branded, HIPAA-compliant app without building from scratch. Licensing is the monetization model that turns a single-product company into a platform.

The Licensing Landscape for Mobile Apps

Licensing in the mobile app context goes beyond traditional software licensing. It encompasses three distinct patterns:

PatternWhat it isWho paysExample
Enterprise LicensingLicense the app to an institution for their employees/members/patientsEmployer, hospital, university, governmentHospital licensing a chronic care app for discharged patients
White-Label LicensingLicense the app for rebranding under the licensee’s brandAny brand wanting an app without developmentTelecom company white-labeling a meditation app as “Wellness by [Carrier]“
API/SDK LicensingLicense functionality as an API or SDK for embedding in other productsDevelopers, integratorsTwilio licensing communications API; Stripe licensing payments

The common thread: the app developer retains IP but grants usage rights to a licensee who pays for access, typically on a recurring basis.

Enterprise Licensing: The B2B Revenue Model

How It Works

Consumer app developer
       ↓
"License my app to your patient population"
       ↓
Health system / employer / insurer / government
       ↓
Pays annual license fee (per-user or site-flat)
       ↓
Deploys app to their members/employees/patients
       ↓
Members access app (often via SSO / existing portal)

Revenue Models

Per-User Licensing:

  • $X per active user per month/year
  • Common in healthcare: “per covered life” or “per enrolled patient”
  • Example: $2-5 per patient per month for care coordination apps
  • Predictable: revenue scales with licensee’s user base

Site/Fleet Licensing (Flat Fee):

  • Fixed annual fee regardless of user count
  • Example: 50,000−200,000/year for unlimited users
  • Attractive to large organizations: caps their cost
  • Developer revenue is capped but predictable

Tiered Enterprise Licensing:

  • Bronze: up to 1,000 users at $X/user/month
  • Silver: up to 10,000 users at lower $/user/month
  • Gold: unlimited users at fixed annual fee
  • Scales with organization size

Why Enterprises Pay

DriverExplanation
Cost avoidanceBuilding the app would cost $500K-5M+; licensing costs a fraction
Speed to deployEnterprise can deploy in weeks instead of 12-24 months of development
ComplianceApp already has HIPAA, SOC2, or relevant certifications
IntegrationApp already integrates with EHR, HR systems, or other enterprise software
Brand controlEnterprise can customize branding, UX, and sometimes functionality
Risk transferLicensee offloads app development and maintenance risk to licensor

White-Label Licensing: License to the Licensee

White-label licensing is distinct from enterprise licensing: the licensee rebrands the app entirely and deploys it as their own product. The end user may not know the app was licensed.

How It Works

App developer
       ↓
"White-label this app under your brand"
       ↓
Brand (telecom, bank, retailer, healthcare system)
       ↓
Pays setup fee + recurring license fee
       ↓
Brand deploys app as "[Brand] Wellness" or "[Brand] Care"
       ↓
End users see only the brand's name and logo

Revenue Components

ComponentTypical RangeNotes
Setup/customization fee10,000−100,000One-time; covers branding, SSO, custom features
Per-user monthly fee0.50−5.00/user/monthLower than direct-to-consumer pricing
Revenue share0-20% of in-app purchasesOptional; sometimes negotiated
Annual maintenance5,000−50,000/yearSupport, updates, infrastructure

Why White-Label Licensees Pay

  • Telecoms: Differentiate from competitors; offer “wellness” or “parental controls” without development
  • Banks: Offer financial health or fraud protection tools under their brand
  • Healthcare systems: Deploy patient engagement apps under their own brand (Epic MyChart model)
  • Retailers: Offer branded fitness, nutrition, or shopping apps
  • Employers: White-label wellness apps as part of EAP (Employee Assistance Program)

Healthcare: The Killer Use Case

Healthcare is the most compelling category for app licensing. The structural reasons:

  1. Clear institutional buyer: hospitals, health systems, insurers, employers all have budget and incentive to improve patient outcomes
  2. Regulatory compliance barrier: HIPAA compliance is hard; licensing a compliant app is easier than building
  3. Integration requirement: apps need EHR integration (Epic, Cerner); integration is a moat
  4. Long sales cycles create switching costs: once deployed, a hospital system won’t switch easily
  5. Value-based care incentives: under value-based care, providers are paid for outcomes, not visits — they need patient engagement tools

Concrete Healthcare Licensing Examples

Epic MyChart (best-known example):

  • Epic licenses MyChart patient portal to health systems
  • Health systems deploy to patients for appointment scheduling, test results, messaging
  • Revenue model: bundled into Epic’s EHR licensing (site-based)
  • This is the gold standard: embedded in the EHR = hospital can’t easily leave

Omada Health:

  • Licenses its diabetes prevention program to employers and health plans
  • Employers pay per employee enrolled
  • Demonstrates outcomes (reduced diabetes incidence) →续费
  • B2B2C: consumer uses the app, employer pays

Hinge Health:

  • Musculoskeletal care coordination app
  • Licensed to employers as an employee benefit
  • Revenue: per-covered-employee per month
  • Outcome guarantees: if members don’t show improvement, some fees refunded

Livongo (now Teladoc Health):

  • Chronic condition management (diabetes, hypertension, weight management)
  • Licensed to employers and health plans
  • Per-user per month model
  • Data-driven: collects biometric data → demonstrates value → justifies renewal

API/SDK Licensing: Licensing to Developers

Apps with valuable core functionality can license that functionality as an API or SDK to other developers. This is a form of licensing that converts consumer features into B2B revenue.

Examples

  • Twilio: Communications API — any app can add SMS, voice, video by licensing Twilio’s API
  • Stripe: Payments API — any app can add payments by licensing Stripe
  • Spotify: Music streaming SDK — any app can embed Spotify’s music library
  • Google Maps: Maps and location API — any app can embed maps by licensing Google Maps
  • Firebase: Authentication, database, analytics SDK — any app can license Firebase

Revenue Model

ModelHow it worksExample
