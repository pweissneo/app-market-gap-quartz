Monetisation - Fee-For-Outcomes Medical Billing Navigation

Medical billing advocates charge 25–35% of savings as contingency fees. 70% of insurance claim appeals succeed when pursued, yet 95% of patients don’t appeal because the process is daunting. A fee-for-outcomes model aligns the app’s incentive perfectly with the patient’s: the app only earns when it saves the patient money or preserves their credit. This is the most direct application of pay-for-outcomes in consumer finance — and the only category where the outcome (a denied claim reversed, a billing error corrected, a debt规避) is fully verifiable from public records.

How the Model Works

Core principle: The app earns a fee only when it achieves a measurable, verifiable outcome for the user. The user pays nothing (or a small base fee) upfront; the app’s revenue comes from a percentage of money saved or a flat success fee.

Three variants in medical billing:

VariantHow it chargesUser upfront costApp earns when
Contingency (% of savings)25–35% of amount saved$0Claim reversed, error corrected, debt reduced
Flat success fee50–200 per successful outcome0–25 deferredAppeal wins, bill corrected, debt resolved
Hybrid (base + contingency)$5–15/month base + 15–25% of savingsLow monthly baseAny successful outcome

Why this fits medical billing uniquely:

  1. Outcomes are objectively verifiable — a claim denial can be shown as reversed; a billing error can be shown as corrected; a debt can be shown as reduced or eliminated
  2. The financial impact is large — medical bills can be thousands or tens of thousands of dollars; a 30% contingency on a 12,000successfulappeal=3,600 earned
  3. The user has no risk — if the app fails, the user owes nothing; the app bears the cost of doing the work
  4. The outcome is time-bounded — insurance appeal deadlines are typically 30–60 days; medical debt goes to collections in 30–90 days; the app must act fast, creating urgency

Industry Precedent

Medical Billing Advocates (Human Services)

The offline analog is well-established:

  • Medical billing advocate services charge 25–35% of amount saved as contingency
  • Some charge flat fees (200–500 per bill review) or hourly (150–300/hour)
  • Success rate for claim appeals: 70% when pursued
  • Market: patients spend 14 hours/year dealing with medical billing paperwork; 41% have medical debt

Key players:

  • Medical Billing Helper (contingency model)
  • ClaimMapper (subscription + contingency)
  • HealthJoy (employer-sponsored, hybrid model) -_stride (app-based, subscription)

   ↓

   ↓

The Engagement Loop

Unlike subscription apps where engagement is measured by daily opens, fee-for-outcomes apps are event-driven:

  1. Bill received → user joins with a specific case
  2. Case active → app sends reminders, deadline alerts, progress updates
  3. Outcome achieved → user gets notification of win, app charges success fee
  4. Case closed → app prompts for review, asks to share, offers next-case discount

Key insight: The app doesn’t need daily engagement to earn revenue. It needs case volume and a high success rate. This is structurally different from every other app category.

The Churn Paradox

Subscription apps measure churn as monthly cancellations. Fee-for-outcomes apps have a different problem: users go quiet after winning. A user who had a 15,000claimreversedandpaid4,500 in contingency fees is unlikely to return unless they get another bill.

This is not churn in the traditional sense — it’s case-based engagement. The app needs a pipeline of new cases, not retention of existing users.

Mitigation: Build a “monitoring” feature that checks the user’s insurance claims proactively, alerting them to potential issues before they become bills. This creates ongoing engagement without requiring the user to remember they have the app.

Platform Considerations

iOS App Store

  • Apps cannot charge contingency fees directly via App Store IAP (Apple prohibits “fee earned only if outcome achieved” as it may constitute gambling/lottery mechanics)
  • Workaround: Use a web-based transaction flow where the app directs users to a web portal for case submission and payment processing, keeping payment processing outside the App Store
  • Alternatively: offer subscription + optional “success pack” IAP that activates the contingency feature
  • Alternative billing in EU (DMA): allows third-party payment processors, potentially enabling direct contingency billing on iOS in EU

Android Play Store

  • More flexible payment options; direct contingency billing more feasible
  • Google Play’s better CPM for ad-supported features is less relevant for this model
  • No specific restriction on outcome-based pricing

Hybrid Approach

To navigate platform restrictions while capturing both revenue streams:

FREE TIER: Bill upload + error detection + appeal template generation
   ↓
SUBSCRIPTION TIER ($9.99/month): Active case management, deadline tracking, negotiation coaching
   ↓
SUCCESS FEE (billed off-platform): 25% of amount saved on successful appeals — user agrees to terms in web flow

This structure: subscription for ongoing service, contingency for one-time case wins. The subscription covers the work; the contingency covers the big win.
