Hypothesis - Wallet and Card Expiration — No App Tracks Card Renewals and Updates Automatically

1.2B credit cards in US. Average person has 4 credit/debit cards. But there’s no app that tracks card expiration dates, predicts when new cards arrive, provides a unified card dashboard, auto-updates subscriptions, monitors for fraud, or tracks physical card location. “When does my card expire?” “Update card for Netflix.” The gap: no wallet and card expiration tracking app.

The Six Core Pains

1. Card Expiration Tracking

The failure:

  • “When does my card expire?”
  • No expiration tracking
  • “I found an expired card in my drawer”

What should exist:

  • “Card expiration tracker” — never be surprised
  • Expiration calendar: “Visa ending 4242: expires June 2027. Renewal alert: 60 days before.”
  • Card details: “All 4 cards: expiration dates, card type, last 4 digits. On file.”
  • Auto-update: “When new card arrives: info updated. Subscription alerts: sent.”

2. Renewal Timing

The failure:

  • “New card arrives when?”
  • No delivery date prediction
  • “I don’t know when my new card comes”

What should exist:

  • “Delivery tracker” — know when cards arrive
  • Processing status: “New card: processing. Estimated delivery: 5-7 business days.”
  • Tracking: “Card shipped: via USPS. Tracking number: attached.”
  • Arrival prediction: “Expected arrival: Friday. Alert: 1 day before.”
  • Activation reminder: “Card arrived. Activate now. Tap to activate.”

3. Unified Card Dashboard

The failure:

  • “All my cards in one place”
  • No unified card dashboard
  • “I have to check each bank app”

What should exist:

  • “Card dashboard” — all cards, one app
  • Card overview: “4 cards: Visa, Mastercard, Amex, Debit. All on file.”
  • Quick details: “Last 4 digits, expiration, bank. Copy to clipboard.”
  • Card image: “Photo of each card. Front and back. On file.”
  • Security codes: “CVV, PIN: stored securely. Face ID to access.”

4. Subscription Auto-Update

The failure:

  • “Update card for Netflix”
  • No auto-update service
  • “I have to update 15 subscriptions”

What should exist:

  • “Subscription auto-updater” — no more updating
  • Card link: “When new card arrives: auto-link to subscriptions.”
  • Provider sync: “Netflix, Spotify, Amazon: updated automatically.”
  • Exception handling: “This subscription: manual required. Alert: to update.”
  • History: “Updated subscriptions: 12. Failed: 1. Retrying.”

5. Fraud Detection

The failure:

  • “Is my card secure?”
  • No real-time monitoring
  • “I found fraud after the fact”

What should exist:

  • “Card security monitor” — real-time alerts
  • Transaction alerts: “Purchase: $342 at Amazon. Expected? Yes/No.”
  • Unusual activity: “Unusual charge: $1,200. Alert: confirm.”
  • Location alerts: “Charge: Tokyo. You’re in New York. Possible fraud.”
  • Freeze instantly: “Tap to freeze. Transaction blocked. $0 fraud liability.”

6. Physical Location Tracking

The failure:

  • “Where is my new card?”
  • No card tracker
  • “The card arrived but I can’t find it”

What should exist:

  • “Card finder” — never lose a card
  • Location memory: “Last seen: mailbox. Date: May 1.”
  • Tag integration: “Tile on card: ring nearby. Tap to find.”
  • Alert if lost: “Card detected: not at usual location. Alert sent.”
  • Secure reminder: “Card is in a secure place. Not with wallet.”

Priority: C

Why now: 1.2B credit cards in circulation with average 4 cards per person creates real card management burden; no app combines expiration tracking, delivery prediction, and subscription auto-update in one place.

Biggest risk: Apple Wallet and Google Pay add expiration notification features, commoditizing the category. Also, Apple Pay/Google Pay reduce physical card usage, potentially shrinking the addressable market.
