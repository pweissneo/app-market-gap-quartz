Monetisation - Usage-Based and Pay-Per-Use

Usage-based pricing aligns cost with value delivery, eliminating the “I’m not using it enough to justify the subscription” problem. But AI has disrupted the model: DeepSeek’s free-only consumer tier forces a re-examination of whether any subscription floor is defensible against capable free competition. Meanwhile, AI power tiers have established $200-300/month as a viable ceiling — unprecedented in consumer apps.

Pattern Definition

Usage-based (pay-per-use): users pay in proportion to consumption — per API call, per document processed, per minute of AI usage, per scanned receipt. Distinct from pure IAP (consumable credits) because the meter runs continuously against a known unit price. The dominant model for AI-native apps since 2024.

Why Usage-Based Is Growing Now

  • Subscription fatigue: users feel anxiety about unused subscriptions; usage-based aligns cost with value
  • AI era: Large Language Model (LLM) and image generation APIs are inherently metered; apps building on these must pass costs through or absorb them in subscription tiers
  • Value alignment: User pays when they get value, not when they remember to open an app
  • Lower entry barrier: No large upfront commitment; users can start free and spend as they extract value
  • Platform arbitrage: EU DMA enables alternative payment providers on iOS in Europe, making usage-based billing without Apple’s 30% cut viable in EU markets

Four Sub-Patterns

Sub-Pattern 1: API-Credit Meter (AI Apps)

How it works:

  • Free tier: X tokens/requests per day or month
  • Paid tiers: flat subscription with usage credits, or metered by usage above threshold
  • Examples: ChatGPT (20/moPluswithmessagelimits),Claude(20/mo Pro, ~45 msgs/5hr), Perplexity ($20/mo Pro, 300+ searches/day)

The AI pricing ceiling (September 2026):

  • AI power tiers now reach 200−300/month—nolongerexceptional,nowestablished:ChatGPTPro(100-200/mo), Claude Max (100−200/mo),PerplexityMax(200/mo), Grok Heavy ($300/mo)
  • The 20/monthfloorisnearlyuniversalforstandardtiers—butGeminiat4.99/mo and DeepSeek at free-only have disrupted it
  • Gap: No consumer habit tracker or productivity app has successfully implemented AI-native usage-based pricing, but the AI pricing playbook is now well-documented

DeepSeek disruption (mid-2026):

  • DeepSeek V4-Pro: completely free, unlimited usage, no ads, no paywall
  • No paid consumer plan — only API access is paid
  • Forces a re-examination: general-purpose AI is now free at a capable level; vertical differentiation is required to justify any subscription floor
  • Implication: a general AI assistant cannot sustain $20/mo against DeepSeek; a specialized vertical AI app can, because users pay for domain expertise not just “AI access”

Platform asymmetry:

  • iOS: Apple restrictions on external payment make AI overage billing complex; Apple’s 30% cut applies to in-app purchases
  • Android: Google Play allows external billing for non-Google AI services in some regions; EU DMA enables alternative payment providers on iOS in Europe

Sub-Pattern 2: Document/Action Consumption

How it works:

  • Free: process N documents per month
  • Paid: per-document cost above threshold
  • Examples: Receipt scanners (expense management), document signers, PDF converters, language translators

Conversion dynamics:

  • Free users who convert hit a concrete pain point (they need N+1 documents)
  • Conversion moment is natural and forced — no artificial scarcity needed
  • Risk: power users flee to competitors if per-unit price is too high

Sub-Pattern 3: Time-Metered Access

How it works:

  • Pay per minute/hour of usage
  • Examples: VPN services (per-hour data caps), language learning apps (per-lesson), meditation apps (per-session)

Key insight: Time-metering works when the session has a clear start/end and the value is experienced in-session. Poor fit for apps where value accumulates silently (habit tracking, note-taking).

Sub-Pattern 4: Hybrid: Subscription Floor + Usage Overage

How it works:

  • Base subscription covers typical usage with a credit allowance
  • Overage charges kick in above a threshold, OR a higher tier removes the limit entirely
  • This is the dominant consumer AI model: ChatGPT Plus (20/mo,messagelimits),ClaudePro(20/mo, ~45 msgs/5hr), Perplexity Pro ($20/mo, 300+ searches/day)

Why it wins:

  • Predictable floor for developer revenue
  • Aligns with users who want commitment without fear of runaway costs
  • Eliminates the “I don’t know what I’ll pay” anxiety of pure usage-based
  • The subscription IS the usage-based layer — the credit allowance is the meter

September 2026 pricing reality check:

  • The 20/mofloorisstandardbutnolongeruniversal(GeminiPlusat4.99/mo disrupts it)
  • DeepSeek has no consumer subscription at all — free only
  • Power tiers at $100-200/mo are now established and common across major providers
  • Grok Heavy at $300/mo is the current consumer AI ceiling

The Conversion Problem

Usage-based has a fundamental weakness: no natural paywall moment for AI queries.

ModelNatural conversion trigger
FreemiumUser hits feature/usage limit
Hard paywallFirst open — immediate
Usage-based (pure)None — user must decide to pay before experiencing value
Hybrid floor+creditsUser hits message/search cap and needs more NOW

What drives AI conversion (15-20% vs 8% SaaS median):

  • The free tier genuinely limits capability (queue times, model restrictions, daily caps) — not just fewer features
  • The value proposition is immediate: “pay $20 to not wait in line”
  • AI has created a new cost expectation in consumer minds: “AI costs money to run”
  • DeepSeek’s free tier sets a high bar for what “free” means — conversion requires demonstrably more value than capable free alternatives

Workarounds:

  1. Free credit allowance: give tokens/credits upfront; conversion happens when they run out
  2. Hybrid floor: subscription covers typical usage; overage is the usage-based element
  3. Deep research/file analysis features gated to paid — these feel premium and worth paying for
  4. Response quality degradation on free tier (slower model, longer queues) creates felt urgency

The Subscription Cannibalisation Risk

ScenarioRisk LevelMitigation
Power users who would pay $15/mo flatHighOffer a “unlimited” subscription tier above usage-based
Casual users who spend $2/monthLowThey wouldn’t subscribe anyway
Enterprise/team usersNoneEnterprise plans should always be subscription

Key insight: Usage-based and subscription serve different user segments. The app should offer both, with subscription framed as “peace of mind” and usage-based as “pay only for what you use.” For AI apps, the hybrid floor+credit model collapses the distinction — the subscription IS the usage-based layer.

EU Digital Markets Act (DMA) creates a structural pricing arbitrage: iOS apps in the EU can use alternative payment providers, making usage-based billing without Apple’s 30% cut viable in EU markets. This creates a pricing advantage for EU users and may drive demand for similar pricing elsewhere.

Gap Implications for App Builders

  1. AI apps should lead with hybrid floor+credits: ChatGPT set the expectation; users in AI apps expect to pay by consumption, but a flat subscription with generous limits is more predictable
  2. The subscription floor is eroding for general AI: DeepSeek has made general-purpose AI free at a capable level; vertical differentiation is now required to justify $9.99-19.99/month
  3. Document/action apps have natural conversion: implement free tier by action count, upgrade when they hit limit
  4. Hybrid floor + overage is the safest structure: predictable revenue floor, upside for engaged users
  5. Avoid pure usage-based for engagement apps: habit trackers, journaling apps, meditation apps — value is cumulative, not session-based
  6. iOS usage-based requires careful billing architecture: factor Apple revenue share into overage pricing; EU DMA provides an alternative path in Europe
