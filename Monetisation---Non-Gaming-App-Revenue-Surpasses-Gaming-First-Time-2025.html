Monetisation - Non-Gaming App Revenue Surpasses Gaming First Time 2025

In 2025, consumers spent 84.1Bonnon−gamingappsvs82.7B on games — the first time apps collectively outpaced gaming in total in-app consumer spend. But the headline obscures as much as it reveals: power-law concentration means the top 1% of publishers drive 90%+ of store revenue, off-store monetization (ads, web stores, B2B) is excluded from these figures and skews heavily toward gaming, and the US crossed this threshold three years earlier than global markets. The shift is real, but it changes what the gap means for indie developers vs platform-level investors.

Critical Nuances (RevenueCat Analysis)

The milestone is real but the implications are more complex than headlines suggest.

1. Concentration: Top 1% Drives 90%+ of Revenue

Store revenue follows extreme power-law distribution. The top 1% of publishers generate over 90% of app store revenue. The $84.1B in non-gaming revenue is overwhelmingly driven by a handful of giants — TikTok, YouTube, Disney+, HBO Max, ChatGPT, Google One — not by the hundreds of thousands of independent developers reading this headline.

Implication for monetization strategy: The “apps > games” milestone reflects the success of platform-level winners. It does not indicate that building a successful independent non-gaming app has become easier or more profitable than building a successful independent game. The monetization dynamics at the indie level remain brutal for both.

2. Market-Level Timing Varies Widely

The global milestone was reached in 2025. But in the US, non-gaming spend surpassed gaming as early as late 2022/2023. In India, the same shift occurred years earlier. Meanwhile, South Korea (73% gaming in 2024), Japan (67%), and Germany remain heavily gaming-dominated.

Implication: A US-based indie developer targeting US users already operated in a world where apps outperformed games. The global headline is trailing indicator for established markets, not a new signal.

3. Revenue Figures Exclude the Largest Monetization Streams

Store-reported figures only capture in-app purchases and subscriptions via the two app stores. They exclude:

  • In-app advertising — estimated ~$390B in 2025, which is ~2.5x larger than the IAP market and heavily gaming-skewed (hypercasual games run entirely on ads)
  • Off-store web monetization — growing fast for both games (web stores for VIP whales) and apps (web2app funnels, CRM)
  • Physical goods and services — Apple/Analysis Group data showed over 1Tinphysicalgoodssoldthroughapps,150B in advertising, and $131B in digital goods (the last being the only segment captured in store revenue)
  • B2B and enterprise revenue — not reflected in consumer spend figures

Implication: Gaming is significantly larger than the store-revenue comparison suggests because its ad monetization is proportionally much larger and largely uncounted in these figures. The gap this milestone marks is real but structurally undercounted for games.

4. Gaming’s Problem Is Stagnation, Not Decline

Gaming revenue is not falling — it is flat to low-single-digit growth. The apps > games shift is primarily driven by non-gaming’s continued rapid growth (20.8% YoY) rather than gaming contracting. The ceiling for mobile gaming has likely been reached in saturated markets; the ceiling for subscription and AI apps has not.

What This Means for App Monetization Strategy

For Non-Gaming App Developers

The structural shift validates the subscription and IAP model in non-gaming categories. The key implications:

  1. Subscription monetization is now proven at scale across categories — entertainment, productivity, health, education, AI. Any non-gaming app with ongoing value delivery has a viable subscription path.

  2. iOS is the premium monetization platform — iOS revenue grew 11.1% vs Google Play’s 5.3%. iOS users account for ~70% of consumer spend. If you’re building a subscription-first non-gaming app, iOS-first launch remains the right sequencing for revenue validation.

  3. AI apps are a new major revenue category — ChatGPT’s rapid ascent to top-grossing charts signals that AI-powered subscription apps are now a top-tier non-gaming category. The willingness to pay for AI-powered features is proven.

  4. The indie opportunity is in niches, not broad categories — The broad categories (social, entertainment, productivity suites) are dominated by incumbents with massive user bases and brand recognition. Independent opportunity exists at category edges: specialized vertical tools, niche community apps, hyper-specific productivity apps.

For Gaming Developers

The data does not mean gaming is dying. Key points:

  1. Hyper-casual gaming’s ad revenue is not reflected — the ad market that funds most hyper-casual games ($390B ad market, largely game-funded) is bigger than the IAP market and growing. Gaming’s true total monetization is larger than these figures suggest.

  2. Mid-core and hardcore games remain IAP-dominant — These segments have strong payer conversion and high ARPU from dedicated players. The shift is less relevant here.

  3. Cross-development consideration — Studios considering porting to non-gaming categories have a structural tailwind: non-gaming subscription revenue is growing 20%+ YoY while gaming is stagnant.
