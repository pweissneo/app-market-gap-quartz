Monetisation - One-Time Purchase vs Subscription Mobile Performance Comparison 2026

Subscriptions grow at +105% YoY. One-time purchase resurging in professional/productive categories. But 62% of consumers feel subscription-overwhelmed. Which model actually wins for mobile apps in 2026 — and under what conditions? This study directly compares the two dominant monetisation patterns across WTP, LTV, conversion, churn, and developer economics, with evidence from AppsFlyer, Sensor Tower, Adapty, and Business of Apps.

Pattern Definitions

Subscription (IAS): Recurring fee (weekly/monthly/annual) for ongoing access. Revenue back-loaded: only 28% of Day-60 revenue arrives on Day 1. Day-7 proxy underestimates true LTV by ~48%. Primary acquisition is organic (70%) for non-gaming subscription apps.

One-Time Purchase (IAP): Single upfront payment for perpetual access. Revenue front-loaded. Requires ongoing paid updates or follow-on products for sustainable revenue. Higher friction at conversion, but no recurring commitment for users.

Dutch Model (Hybrid): Upfront purchase grants perpetual ownership; optional paid updates after 12 months. Combines low friction acquisition with ongoing revenue from update-needers.

Conversion and Acquisition

Conversion Rates

Subscription conversion is harder but more durable:

  • Free-to-paid subscription conversion: typically 2–5% for well-designed paywalls (AppsFlyer benchmark)
  • One-time purchase conversion: typically 1–3% for paid apps (App Store data)
  • Subscription wins on revenue per converted user despite lower conversion rate

Subscription apps grow organically; IAP apps depend on paid acquisition. This has major implications:

  • Subscription apps need strong ASO, content marketing, and product-led growth
  • Paid acquisition for subscription apps has poor ROI (users want to try before they commit)
  • IAP apps can buy users directly if the LTV/CAC math works

The fatigue is real but asymmetric. It is most acute for:

  • Low-engagement, low-value subscriptions (“I forgot I had this”)
  • Services competing in crowded categories (multiple streaming services)
  • Professional tools where users want ownership, not rental

It is least acute for:

  • High-engagement, daily-use apps ( Spotify, Netflix)
  • AI-powered apps with ongoing value delivery
  • Social/communication apps with network effects

Subscription retention is structurally low. Most subscribers churn within 30 days. But the subscribers who survive to Day 90 generate outsized LTV — the back-loading means a small retention cohort carries most of the revenue.

Churn Mitigation: Pause/Resume

The pause model (see Monetisation - Subscription Pause and Resume Model) reduces churn 9.8% and lifts LTV 1.4x. 68% of users who pause resume within 3 months and spend 22% more than never-paused subscribers.

For one-time purchase apps: There is no recurring revenue to lose, but there is also no recurring engagement hook. One-time purchasers who churn from engagement are gone permanently.

The Dutch Model: Hybrid That Captures Both

Best Dutch Model mobile examples: Notion shifted to tiered subscription but offers one-time “personal” purchase. Obsidian offers lifetime license. Linear offers both. The pattern is more established in productivity/professional tools than in consumer apps.

Dutch Model revenue math:

  • 120one−time+70/year update renewal = effective ~$70/year over 3 years
  • Compare to 120/yearsubscription=360 over 3 years
  • Dutch Model user pays 190totalvs360 — but many users never pay again, reducing effective ARPU
  • Trade-off: Dutch Model wins on acquisition (low friction), subscription wins on LTV from committed users

Decision Matrix: Which Model to Choose

Choose Subscription When:

  • App delivers ongoing, daily/weekly value (not a one-time need)
  • Target user is a committed professional willing to pay for ongoing value
  • You have organic acquisition channels (ASO, content, community)
  • App has AI/live services that require ongoing computation cost
  • You need predictable MRR for investor or operational planning
  • Category norm is subscription (productivity, health, streaming, education)

Choose One-Time Purchase When:

  • App solves a one-time problem (搬家, wedding planning, document creation)
  • Target market has high CapEx tolerance (pro tools, creative apps)
  • App has no ongoing costs (local-only, no server, no live features)
  • You are targeting power users who distrust subscriptions
  • Category norm is one-time purchase (utilities, utilities, some games)
  • You want maximum acquisition reach in price-sensitive markets

Choose Dutch Model When:

  • App has a clear v1.0 vs v2.0 distinction (major version capability difference)
  • Target users are pro/small business (ownership-oriented, budget-conscious)
  • You want hybrid acquisition (low friction + ongoing revenue)
  • Your ongoing costs are low (updates are marginal effort, not server costs)
  • Category is productivity/professional tools (where Dutch Model is recognized)

Platform and Billing Policy Considerations

Apple App Store

  • Standard 15–30% Apple cut on all purchases
  • Subscription takes 15% after 12 consecutive months of paid subscription (reduced rate)
  • One-time purchase takes 15–30% depending on revenue tier
  • Hybrid billing (one-time + subscription) is allowed but must use In-App Purchase for digital goods/services
  • Physical goods and non-App-Store services can use external payment processors
  • EU Digital Markets Act (DMA): Alternative payment processors allowed in EU since 2024, reducing Apple cut to 17% (17% tech fee + 0.50€/user/year) or standard IAP

Google Play

  • Standard 15% on first $1M revenue, 30% above (effective 15% average across all developers)
  • Subscription takes 15% after 12 months (same as Apple)
  • One-time purchase takes standard 15–30%
  • User Choice Billing in EU/South Korea: allows alternative billing, developer pays 26% (24% + 0.50€/user/year)
  • No equivalent to Apple’s 3-year reduced subscription rate yet
