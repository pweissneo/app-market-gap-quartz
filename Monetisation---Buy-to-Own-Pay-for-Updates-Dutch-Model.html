Monetisation - Buy-to-Own Pay-for-Updates (Dutch Model)

Subscription fatigue is real: 62% of consumers feel overwhelmed by subscriptions (Adapty, 2025/2026). The Buy-to-Own Pay-for-Updates model — the “Dutch Model” — offers a middle path: users own their purchase outright, with optional paid updates after 12 months. Proven profitable for professional tools since 2015 (JetBrains) and adopted by design tools (Sketch). For mobile apps with subscription fatigue in 2026, this model opens a gap where traditional subscriptions are rejected.

Pattern Definition

The Buy-to-Own Pay-for-Updates model (aka “Dutch Model” after Netherlands consumer protection origins) works as follows:

  1. Upfront purchase — user pays a one-time fee to own the current version forever
  2. 12-month update window — updates included free for the first year
  3. Optional renewal — after 12 months, user can pay an update fee to access new features
  4. Perpetual ownership — even without renewal, the purchased version continues to work

This contrasts with:

  • Pure subscription: Access only while paying; cancel = lose access
  • Pure one-time purchase: Never pay again; no revenue after initial sale
  • Dutch Model: Own forever; pay again for major updates

How It Works

PURCHASE: $120 one-time
├── Own current version forever
├── Free updates for 12 months
└── After 12 months: optional $50-80/year renewal for updates

RUNTIME BEHAVIOR:
├── Year 1: Full features, full updates — $120 spent
├── Year 2: Continue using v1.X without updates — $0 additional
├── Year 3: Pay $70 to update to v2.X, get updates until Year 4 — $70
└── Repeat: Pay to update every 1-3 years as needed

Why “Dutch Model”

The term emerged from Dutch consumer law requiring sellers to clearly disclose that products purchased at a one-time price remain usable even if the seller discontinues them or the user doesn’t renew subscriptions. This protection gave rise to the naming convention in European software circles.

Sketch’s Business Result

From their pricing page: “We’ve been profitable since day one. Our business model is simple — we sell our product at a fair price.”

Key observations:

  • No free tier — “We’ve been profitable since day one”
  • One-time price equals one-year subscription price ($120)
  • One-time buyer gets exactly 1 year of updates
  • After year 1, one-time buyer can either pay an update fee or continue using the owned version
  • Sketch does not publicly disclose the renewal/update pricing for one-time purchasers

What Makes Sketch’s Model Work

  1. Professional audience — designers have budget and need stable tools
  2. Offline capability — the app works fully without cloud dependency
  3. No server costs for core product — local documents = no per-user hosting cost
  4. Collaboration as premium feature — single-user Mac app is free, collaboration requires paid plan

Case Study: JetBrains (Developer IDEs)

Model: Subscription with perpetual fallback license after 12 months

JetBrains implemented a hybrid version of the Dutch Model:

JetBrains Licensing Structure

For their IDEs (IntelliJ IDEA, PyCharm, etc.):

ComponentDescription
SubscriptionMonthly or annual payment for access + updates
Perpetual fallbackAfter 12 consecutive months of subscription, user gets a perpetual license for that version
Continuity discountRenewing subscribers get up to 40% off

Key JetBrains Innovation: Perpetual Fallback License

After 12 months of paying:

  • You own a perpetual license for the version at month 12
  • You can cancel subscription and still use that version forever
  • If you want updates, you re-subscribe (at continuity discount)

This creates a “savings account” of perpetual licenses:

Year 1: Pay $150, own v2024.1 forever
Year 2: Pay $90 (40% continuity discount), own v2025.1 forever
Year 3: Pay $90, own v2026.1 forever
...
After cancellation: You have v2026.1 perpetual + all prior versions

JetBrains for On-Premises Tools

For YouTrack Server and TeamCity On-Premises, JetBrains uses “Perpetual licensing with upgrade subscription”:

  • Pay once for perpetual license + 1 year of updates
  • Optionally renew annually for continued updates
  • This is the pure Dutch Model

The Mobile App Opportunity

Why the Dutch Model is Emerging in 2026

  1. Subscription fatigue: 62% of consumers overwhelmed by subscriptions (Adapty)
  2. App Store billing pressure: Apple/Google take 15-30% of subscriptions but fewer restrictions on one-time
  3. DMA enforcement: EU pressure on Apple/Google for more flexible billing
  4. Consumer ownership demand: High-profile cases of digital storefronts revoking access made users wary

Mobile App Specific Considerations

FactorImplication for Mobile Dutch Model
App Store policyApple/Google prefer subscriptions; one-time purchase is allowed but less promoted
Server costsApps with server-side features (sync, AI, cloud storage) struggle with Dutch Model — updates often tied to server costs
Update cadenceMobile apps typically update more frequently than desktop tools — may need lower update price or longer windows
AI featuresAI model updates may not align with app update cycle — separate AI subscription may be necessary

Categories Where Dutch Model Could Work

  • Productivity apps (note-taking, writing, task management) — offline-first, infrequent major releases
  • Utility apps (calculators, converters, reference tools) — one-time value proposition
  • Professional tools (design, video editing, audio) — users have budget and update when significant
  • Education apps — schools prefer owning licenses vs subscriptions

Categories Where Dutch Model Struggles

  • Social/communication apps — network effects require ongoing subscription
  • AI-powered apps — model updates are costly and frequent
  • Gaming — ongoing content creation requires subscription
  • Cloud-dependent apps — server costs don’t disappear with one-time purchase

The Gap for Mobile Apps

Current Problem

Most productivity and utility apps use:

  • Freemium (converts at 2-5%) or
  • Subscription (rejected by subscription-fatigued users)

Opportunity

The Dutch Model for mobile apps would:

  1. Price at 1-year subscription equivalent as one-time purchase
  2. Include 12-18 months of updates
  3. Offer renewal at 50-60% of original price for subsequent updates
  4. Allow continued use of owned version indefinitely

Example pricing for a productivity app:

  • One-time: 59.99(equals 5/month × 12)
  • Update renewal: $29.99/year (optional, after first year)
  • Compare to subscription: 5/monthor49/year

What Would Need to Be True

For the Dutch Model to work for a mobile app:

  • App has genuine offline value (no mandatory server dependency)
  • Major updates are meaningful (not just bug fixes)
  • Target user is professional or has professional-level budget
  • Competition uses subscription (so Dutch Model is a differentiator)
  • Update cadence is 12+ months for major versions
