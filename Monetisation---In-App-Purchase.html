Monetisation - In-App Purchase (IAP)

In-app purchase is the dominant monetization model in mobile apps by revenue volume, yet most non-gaming categories underutilize it. Gaming has long been the IAP stronghold, but 2025 marked the first year where non-gaming IAP revenue surpassed gaming — a structural shift that opens new category possibilities. The key to IAP success is episodic value delivery: users buy when they experience a moment of desire, not on a recurring schedule.

Pattern Definition

In-app purchase (IAP): a one-time or repeat digital purchase made inside an app, distinct from subscriptions. IAP encompasses three Apple/Google types:

TypeDescriptionExamples
ConsumableUsed once and depleted; can be repurchasedCoins, gems, power-ups, credits, filters
Non-consumablePurchased once; permanent accessPro unlocks, ad removal, export packs
Auto-renewable subscriptionRecurring billing (technically IAP category but treated separately)Premium tiers, AI credits

Core insight: IAP fits products where value is episodic, collectible, or burst-based — not ongoing access. If the user experience naturally produces “I want that now” moments, IAP works. If value accumulates silently over time (habit tracking, note-taking), subscriptions or freemium typically convert better.

The 2025 Structural Shift: Non-Gaming IAP Surpasses Gaming

For the first time in mobile app history, non-gaming IAP revenue exceeded gaming IAP revenue in 2025, reaching $167 billion total (10.6% YoY growth). This reflects:

  • AI app credits (ChatGPT, Midjourney, Gemini) as consumable IAP
  • Photo/video editing export packs and credits
  • Wellness apps (meditation packs, workout credits)
  • Education apps (course purchases, lesson packs)
  • Travel apps (trip add-ons, guide packages)

Implication: IAP is no longer a gaming-only model. Any app category with burst-based value moments can leverage it.

The Timing Problem: When Do Users Buy?

60% of IAP transactions occur within the first 7 days after install. This means:

  1. First-session impression matters — if users don’t reach a “want” moment quickly, they never convert
  2. Early value hooks are critical — the app must demonstrate its best moment fast
  3. Trial period data shows — 55.4% of 3-day trial cancellations happen on Day 0, 84% between Day 0 and Day 1

For IAP to work, the purchase moment must feel like a natural escalation of value, not a tax on continued access.

The Whale Problem and Why It Matters

IAP economics are extremely skewed:

  • Top 1% of publishers take 93% of IAP revenue
  • Only ~3.5% of users convert, but those who do often spend heavily
  • Gaming whales can spend 100–1000+ per month

For gap discovery: Categories with demonstrated willingness to spend (health, productivity, AI) may be under-monetized via IAP because incumbents haven’t built the right consumable hooks. A habit tracking app with achievement badges and streak extenders (consumable) might convert better than a subscription.

The IAP Design Checklist

  1. Episodic value moments — identify 3–5 moments per session where a user thinks “I want that”
  2. Clear pricing — credit packs should map to obvious value (e.g., $0.99 = 100 credits = 1 export)
  3. First-week conversion window — design onboarding to reach a purchase moment within 3 days
  4. Consumable over permanent — for engagement apps, consumables drive repeat purchases better than permanent unlocks
  5. Frictionless purchase flow — every tap between desire and purchase reduces conversion
  6. Cross-sell to subscription — users who buy credits may also want unlimited access; offer both
