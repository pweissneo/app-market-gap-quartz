Monetisation - Immigration App — TurboTax Model: Tiered Subscription Plus Per-Document

Consumer immigration apps are stuck between free case trackers (low engagement, no revenue) and expensive attorneys ($3,000–10,000+). The gap: an intelligent immigration companion that captures users early, builds trust through free value, and converts on high-stakes moments. The proven model: TurboTax’s tiered subscription + per-document pricing. This note documents how it works, why it fits immigration specifically, and what the revenue benchmarks look like.

Why Immigration Is the TurboTax of Legal Services

Immigration and taxes share a structural feature that made TurboTax possible: high frequency, high stakes, complex paperwork, and a natural spectrum from DIY to professional help.

FeatureTaxesImmigration
ComplexityVery highVery high
FrequencyAnnual (cyclical)Event-driven (case-dependent)
DIY ceilingMedium — tax code is knowableLow — immigration law is harder
Professional cost$150–500/hr$3,000–10,000+ for a case
PaperworkRequired, standardizedRequired, non-standardized
Language barrierLow (English-dominant)High (50M+ non-English speakers)
StakesFinancialLegal residency, deportation
Natural triggerTax seasonReceiving a notice, starting a case

TurboTax solved the tax problem by:

  1. Giving away a free basic tier (simple 1040 filing)
  2. Charging $35–120 for state returns and more complex filings
  3. Selling live human assistance at $80–180/hr
  4. Offering a “max refund guarantee”

An immigration app can mirror this model: free case tracking → tiered subscription for document prep and notices → per-document upsells for high-stakes forms → attorney marketplace for complex cases.

The Three-Tier Monetisation Model

Tier 1 — Free (Case Tracking Only)

What it includes:

  • Track USCIS case status by receipt number
  • Push notifications on case status changes
  • Visa bulletin monitoring
  • Basic document checklist

Purpose: User acquisition at scale. Case Tracker (the dominant app) already has 2M+ users with this model. These users are in the immigration system — they have active cases, upcoming deadlines, and growing anxiety. They are the top-of-funnel.

Why free works: Case status tracking uses publicly available USCIS data (egov.uscis.gov). No value is being provided that isn’t already accessible — it’s a convenience layer. This creates zero CAC but captures the entire at-risk population.

Revenue model: Tier 1 is not directly monetised. Its value is:

  • User base for upselling
  • Trust-building before payment asks
  • App Store visibility (top-100 travel, top-500 productivity)
  • Data on user immigration stage (pre-application, active case, pending, citizenship)

Tier 2 — Subscription (9.99–29.99/month or $79–199/year)

What it includes:

  • Everything in Tier 1, plus:
  • USCIS notice interpreter — AI decodes notice in plain language (RFE, denial warning, appointment notice, etc.)
  • Document preparation assistant — guided help filling out common forms (I-485, I-130, I-765, N-400)
  • Deadline management — proactive alerts 30/14/7/3/1 days before deadlines with action checklists
  • Multi-language support — Spanish and普通话/粤语 at minimum; 10+ languages ideal
  • Document library — secure storage and organisation for all immigration documents
  • Visa eligibility screener — “Based on your situation, you may qualify for: [visa categories]”

Conversion trigger: A user receives a confusing USCIS notice. Their case goes to “Request for Evidence.” They’re terrified. This is the natural payment moment — Tier 2 is the answer to “what do I do now?”

Pricing rationale:

  • 9.99/month=120/year is 1–4% of average attorney hourly rate for a single consultation
  • 199/year=costof1hourwithanimmigrationattorney(vs.3,000–10,000+ for full representation)
  • Comparable: TurboTax Deluxe is 49.99+statefees(35–80); Spotify Family is 16.99/month;Netflixis15.49–22.99/month

Tier 3 — Per-Document Premium (49–299 per form)

What it includes:

  • Full document preparation for a specific immigration form
  • Step-by-step guided completion
  • AI review and error detection
  • “Will this be approved?” confidence assessment (non-legal opinion)

Target forms and pricing:

Tier 4 — Attorney Marketplace (Revenue Share, 15–25% per referral)

What it includes:

  • Curated directory of vetted immigration attorneys
  • DOJ-recognized immigration attorneys only
  • Per-consultation booking (30/60/90 minute sessions)
  • Document review by licensed attorney ($150–300/hour)

Revenue model: 15–25% referral fee on attorney consultations booked through the platform. If an attorney charges 250/consultationand1024/year/user) book one consultation per year → 25/annualuser×100.50/user/year additional revenue.

Why this matters: The app cannot give legal advice. The attorney marketplace is the ethical bridge — it converts users who need professional help into paying customers of vetted attorneys, while keeping the app legally compliant.

The Legal Liability Bridge

Immigration apps face a unique constraint: they cannot provide legal advice. This creates a liability gap that TurboTax doesn’t have (TurboTax can tell you the correct tax form; an immigration app cannot tell you whether you’ll be approved).

The safe framing (DoNotPay failure lesson):

  • ✅ “Here’s what this USCIS notice typically means” (legal information)
  • ✅ “Most people in your situation submit these documents” (general guidance)
  • ❌ “Based on our analysis, you will be approved” (legal advice — illegal without a license)
  • ❌ “You qualify for this visa” (legal advice — requires attorney)

Monetisation implication: The Tier 2/Tier 3 services must be framed as document preparation assistance, not legal advice. This is structurally different from TurboTax (which can give tax advice) but similar to LegalZoom’s document preparation model.

Practical workaround: Attorney marketplace (Tier 4) is where legal advice lives. The app is the “before the lawyer” tool — it prepares users for attorney consultations, organises their case, and ensures they understand what’s happening. The attorney is the high-touch, high-trust, high-revenue upsell.

Churn challenge: Immigration cases are finite. Once a case resolves (approved, denied, or citizenship granted), the user churns. This is different from streaming apps (infinite entertainment). Mitigation: visa renewal market (ongoing), family sponsorship pipeline (new cases), citizenship pipeline (N-400 for existing green card holders).

B2B Revenue Opportunities

1. Law firm white-label ($50K–200K/year per firm)

  • Attorneys and law firms license the app’s notice interpreter and document prep tools
  • White-label: their logo, their client portal, AI-assisted preparation
  • Target: boutique immigration firms (2–20 attorneys) who can’t build this in-house

2. Government / NGO partnerships

  • USCIS could theoretically partner for plain-language notice communication
  • Immigrant-serving NGOs (Catholic Charities, NILC affiliates) could offer the app to clients
  • Nonprofit pricing: $1–5/user/month (low margin, high social impact, grant-funded)

3. Employer immigration portals

  • Large employers (tech companies, hospitals, universities) with H-1B, O-1, green card programs
  • Annual enterprise license: $5–15/employee/month
  • Integration: the app connects to the employer’s immigration tracking
