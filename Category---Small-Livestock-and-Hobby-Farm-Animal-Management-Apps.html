Category - Small Livestock and Hobby Farm Animal Management Apps

Hobby farmers and small-scale livestock keepers (chickens, goats, pigs, sheep, ducks, bees, etc.) need apps to track animal records, health, breeding, egg/produce output, feed schedules, and finances. This category serves non-commercial homesteaders and cottage-industry producers — distinct from commercial ranch management software targeting large herds.

Market Overview

The backyard poultry and small livestock space has grown substantially as urban/suburban hobby farming expanded post-2020. Key platforms cover:

  • Chickens: egg tracking, flock health, breeding records, mortality
  • Goats/sheep: kidding/lambing records, milk production, weight tracking
  • Pigs: growth tracking, breeding cycles, farrowing records
  • Bees: hive inspections, honey production, colony health
  • Multi-species: cross-species record management for diversified homesteads

Monetisation Patterns

  • Subscription (annual): Flockstar at $79.99/yr is the clearest premium pricing signal in poultry
  • Freemium: basic free tier with paid upgrades for multi-flock or financial features
  • One-time purchase: less common in this category; most apps have moved to recurring
  • B2B/enterprise: enterprise tiers for commercial farms (AgriWebb, Mobble)

Competitive Gaps

  1. Cross-species integration is shallow — most apps are single-species or bolt on secondary species as an afterthought. Homesteaders with chickens + goats + bees have no unified option.
  2. Simple chore/task scheduling is underserved — Reddit threads show users resorting to non-app tricks (pill boxes, candy rewards) for basic daily coop tasks. No app dominates simple recurring task reminders tuned for livestock.
  3. Cost-of-production tracking is fragmented — only Flockstar (premium tier) and 123Poultry address cost-per-egg or feed efficiency. Most hobbyists cannot easily answer “am I profitable per dozen eggs?”
  4. Offline-first is underemphasised — many rural hobby farms have poor connectivity; apps requiring constant connectivity are poorly suited.
  5. Small flock/herd minimums don’t fit hobby farms — most apps assume hundreds or thousands of animals; hobby farms with 5-20 animals fall into a gap between pen-and-paper and industrial-scale software.
  6. Specialty animals lack dedicated support — alpaca, llama, emu, heritage breed tracking is underserved; existing apps focus on mainstream cattle, poultry, and swine.
  7. Simple vet record keeping is underbuilt — hobby farmers want just vaccination dates and treatment logs, not full medical chart complexity.
  8. Small-farm marketplace integration absent — no app helps list animals for sale or track sold animals with integration to small-farm marketplaces.
